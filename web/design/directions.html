Beacon — three design directions

Method is production-frontend ch06: five questions, answered before any color exists. Everything below is one component set re-skinned by tokens only — which is exactly how the real app will work. No inline styles anywhere in the product code.

One job of the main screen“What is my team doing right now, and what do I move next?”
Who, under what conditionsA team member, many times a day, 10–90 second sessions, desktop-first.
Density or airThe open question — the three directions answer it differently.
How seriousWork tool, not a bank. Personality allowed; ceremony not.
One memorable thingEach direction proposes its own — the live beam, the paper, the pulse.

1 · Terminal

dark-firstdense · 13px · 5px radius · 80ms

An operations console. The board reads like a well-kept terminal: cool charcoal, cyan signal, everything within one glance. For people who open it forty times a day and want zero ceremony.

The memorable thing: the live wire — SSE presence rendered as a pulsing cyan beacon in the top bar; when a teammate moves a task, you see it breathe.

2 · Atelier

light-firstairy · 15px serif · 10px radius · 200ms

A craftsman's workbench on warm paper. Ink text, cream surfaces, one deep indigo accent. Feels like the Stencil books — calm, literate, deliberate. For a team that thinks of tasks as work worth doing well, not tickets.

The memorable thing: the paper — a warm off-white nobody else ships, serif headings, and completed tasks that get an ink "stamp" animation like a ledger entry being settled.

3 · Pulse

both themes equalfriendly · 14px · 12px radius · 160ms spring

Modern product energy — Linear's discipline with its own voice. Cool neutrals, one vivid violet, confident type, motion that feels alive but never slow. The direction most likely to make someone say "what app is that?"

The memorable thing: the pulse — the beacon logomark sends a ring on every realtime event, and moving a task emits a tiny violet ripple at the drop point.

My recommendation: Pulse (3), stolen sharpness from Terminal.
Take direction 3's palette, logo and motion, but pull the density one notch toward Terminal (14px body, 40px rows, 10px radius). Reasons: the violet is far from all three status hues so the semantics stay clean in both themes; it demos best in a portfolio; and the pulse gimmick is powered by the SSE endpoint your backend actually has — the memorable thing is real, not decoration. Atelier is the most distinctive but its warmth fights the "glanceable console" job of a task board. Say the word — or mix any levers you like: any accent can be swapped onto any density in one token file, which is rather the point.
